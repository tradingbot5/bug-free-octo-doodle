## What major companies run programs on Intigriti? What are their bounty ranges?

### Takeaway
Intigriti (Belgian-founded, now UK-headquartered) hosts around 180 programs total, with approximately 74 confirmed public programs across a broad range of sectors, including notable EU companies, global tech firms, automotive, healthcare, media, and financial services. Critical bounties range from a few hundred euros to $100,000 (Intel).

### Inferences
- Intigriti strongly skews toward European (especially Belgian, Dutch, German, Nordic) companies; US companies like Intel, AMD, Yahoo, Robinhood appear but are less dominant.
- Critical payouts on Intigriti typically range from €1,000–€4,000 for SME/startup programs, up to $100,000 for Intel (the outlier).
- Belgian public-sector and healthcare organizations (Port of Antwerp, UZ Leuven, Water-Link) represent a notable niche absent from US-centric platforms.

### Gaps
- The HuggingFace dataset is a community-maintained snapshot of an uncertain date; some programs may have changed payouts or closed since it was compiled.
- Intigriti's own live program directory was accessible (intigriti.com/programs) but showed only page 1 of 8, limiting full enumeration to what could be scraped.
- KBC's exact bounty tiers on Intigriti were not found; only confirmed the program exists.
- Proximus (Belgian telecom) could not be confirmed as having an Intigriti program from available sources.

## Which programs on Intigriti and YesWeHack are public vs private?

### Takeaway
Both platforms offer public (open to all registered researchers) and private (invite-only) programs; public programs are listed openly, while private programs are invitation-only and not discoverable without an invite.

### Cited Findings
- The HuggingFace dataset captures only **public** programs: 74 Intigriti and 73 YesWeHack — [HuggingFace Bug Bounty Dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/lesis-lat/bug-bounty-programs-rewards/blob/main/programs-data.csv)
- Intigriti's public directory (intigriti.com/programs) is accessible without login and lists public programs. Separate private programs exist and require an invitation. — [Intigriti Programs Page](https://www.intigriti.com/programs)
- Intigriti total program count is approximately 400 (Craft.co, January 2024), of which 110 were public, meaning roughly 290 programs are private — [Craft.co Intigriti Profile](https://craft.co/intigriti)
- YesWeHack explicitly distinguishes "invitation based only (private)" from public programs in their platform description — [CBInsights YesWeHack](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/yeswehack)
- Some Intigriti programs have a public VDP alongside an invite-only paid program (e.g., Mateco VDP lists a "separate private program available on request") — [Intigriti Programs Page](https://www.intigriti.com/programs)
- Grafana Labs on Intigriti: free VDP is public, but a VIP paid bug bounty exists as a separate invite-only tier — [Intigriti Programs Page](https://www.intigriti.com/programs)

### Inferences
- The majority of programs on both platforms are private/invite-only; public programs represent the visible tip of the iceberg.
- Organizations often start private and move to public as their triage capacity matures.

### Gaps
- Private program counts for YesWeHack could not be confirmed; only that "hundreds" of programs exist total.

## Which are paid bug bounty vs free VDP?

### Takeaway
On Intigriti's public directory, approximately 4 programs per page are paid bounty programs and the rest are VDPs; the paid/public bounty programs are better-represented in the searchable dataset. YesWeHack's public dataset shows all 73 listed programs have at least some bounty (some start at €0 for low/medium severity but pay for high/critical).

### Cited Findings
- Of 24 programs on Intigriti's first listing page: 4 were paid bug bounty programs (Adobe, NVIDIA, Daytona, Coveo), 20 were VDPs — [Intigriti Programs Page](https://www.intigriti.com/programs)
- The HuggingFace dataset lists 74 Intigriti programs, all of which include bounty tiers (indicating paid programs); VDP-only programs may not be captured in this dataset — [HuggingFace Bug Bounty Dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/lesis-lat/bug-bounty-programs-rewards/blob/main/programs-data.csv)
- YesWeHack's 73 public programs in the dataset all include at least a critical-tier payout, ranging from $700 (Ooredoo) to €70,000 (Swiss Post e-voting) — [HuggingFace Bug Bounty Dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/lesis-lat/bug-bounty-programs-rewards/blob/main/programs-data.csv)
- YesWeHack's European Commission contract includes both bug bounty programs and vulnerability disclosure policies (VDPs) — [YesWeHack EC News](https://www.yeswehack.com/news/european-commission-tender-won-yeswehack)

### Inferences
- Intigriti appears to have a higher proportion of VDP (non-paying) programs in its public listing, reflecting a large European enterprise/SME market that wants disclosure without bounties.
- YesWeHack's public dataset skews toward paid programs; their government programs tend to be lower-paying but still monetary.

### Gaps
- Exact VDP vs paid split across Intigriti's full ~180 public programs was not available.
- YesWeHack does not publicly publish an aggregate count of VDP vs. paid programs.

## What is the typical scope (web, mobile, API, infrastructure)?

### Takeaway
Both platforms predominantly scope web applications and APIs; mobile apps appear as secondary targets. Infrastructure and source code are less common in public programs but appear in some high-value programs.

### Cited Findings
- Visma's Intigriti program explicitly covers web (URLs/domains) and two mobile apps (iOS and Android), with API endpoints implied by hostnames like api.workbox.dk — [Intigriti Visma Program](https://app.intigriti.com/programs/visma/visma)
- Monzo's Intigriti program covers: website, APIs, internal tooling, and mobile apps — [CSO Online 2024](https://www.csoonline.com/article/4085288/11-top-bug-bounty-programs-launched-in-2024.html)
- Intigriti program pages split assets into in-scope/out-of-scope sections with CVSS v4 scoring — [Apify Intigriti Scraper](https://apify.com/rl1987/intigriti-scraper.md)
- DINUM's YesWeHack program focuses on web apps and APIs — [CSO Online 2024](https://www.csoonline.com/article/4085288/11-top-bug-bounty-programs-launched-in-2024.html)
- Automotive programs (BMW, Skoda, Rivian) typically scope web portals and connected-car APIs rather than firmware/hardware
- Open-source programs (GNOME, systemd, Log4j on YesWeHack) scope source code and software releases

### Inferences
- "Web + API" is the universal default scope on both platforms.
- Mobile is included in most consumer-facing programs (banking apps, food delivery, scooters).
- Infrastructure (cloud, network) scoping appears less frequently, mainly in larger tech company programs.
- Automotive programs on Intigriti (BMW, Skoda) may extend to connected-vehicle APIs but typically exclude in-vehicle firmware.

### Gaps
- No aggregate data on scope breakdown (% web vs mobile vs API vs infrastructure) was found for either platform.

- The EU has chosen to split its bug bounty contracts between Intigriti (earlier open-source programs) and YesWeHack (2025 institutional framework), suggesting both are considered credible EU-sovereign security platforms.
- Belgian critical infrastructure (ports, water utilities, hospitals) is notably active on Intigriti, likely reflecting Belgian regulatory nudges toward vulnerability disclosure.
- French government digitization programs (health pass, identity, transport) are systematically run through YesWeHack, making it the de facto platform for French public digital services.

### Gaps
- Specific bounty tiers for KBC, Orange France, and the French Ministry of Armed Forces programs were not found in public sources.
- Whether Proximus (Belgian telecom, major company) runs an Intigriti program could not be confirmed.
- No confirmed ING, BNP Paribas, Société Générale, or other major European bank programs were found on these platforms in public sources.
