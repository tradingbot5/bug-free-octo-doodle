# Bug Bounties Thrive Beyond HackerOne and Bugcrowd

The most lucrative and mature vulnerability research programs in the world do not run on HackerOne or Bugcrowd. Google paid **$17 million in VRP rewards in 2025** through its own portal, Apple raised its top payout to **$2 million** (with bonus potential above $5 million) on a self-hosted site, and Microsoft distributed **$20 million** to 562 researchers via its own MSRC. Alongside these flagship self-hosted programs, two European platforms — Intigriti and YesWeHack — have carved out a distinct ecosystem of hundreds of paying programs, while Immunefi has built an entirely separate industry for Web3 smart-contract bounties with maximums reaching $10 million. This report maps the full landscape of programs outside HackerOne and Bugcrowd, covering platform, URL, scope, and payout ranges for each.

## Major tech companies built the most capable self-hosted platforms

Google's **Vulnerability Reward Program** at [bughunters.google.com](https://bughunters.google.com/about/rules) is the gold standard for self-hosted programs. The platform covers Android, Chrome, Cloud, and web products under a single roof. After a 2025 overhaul, the top Android zero-click Titan M exploit with persistence now pays **$1.5 million**, while Google Cloud VRP tops out at **$151,515** ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/pro/security/google-bug-bounty-payments-hit-nearly-usd12-million-in-2024)). The program's 2024 largest single payout was $110,000 to one researcher ([Google Security Blog](https://security.googleblog.com/2025/03/vulnerability-reward-program-2024-in.html)).

Apple's program at [security.apple.com/security-bounty](https://security.apple.com/blog/apple-security-bounty-evolved/) sets the highest absolute ceiling in the industry as of November 2025: **$2 million for a zero-click remote attack chain**, $1 million for a one-click remote chain or wireless proximity attack, and a $1,000 floor for low-impact reports. Bonuses for beta-software issues and Lockdown Mode bypasses can push a combined payout above **$5 million**. Coverage spans iOS, iPadOS, macOS, visionOS, watchOS, and tvOS. A notable innovation is "Target Flags," which allow accelerated awards paid before a fix ships.

Microsoft's **Security Response Center** at [msrc.microsoft.com/bounty](https://microsoft.com/en-us/msrc/bounty) covers Cloud Programs (up to $100,000), Endpoint & On-Premises Programs (up to $250,000), and the annual Zero Day Quest hacking contest, which contributed $2.3 million of the $20 million total paid in the period July 2025 to June 2026 ([SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/microsoft-bug-bounty-program-20-million-paid-to-500-researchers/)). A December 2025 "In Scope By Default" policy means any Microsoft-owned vulnerability now has a payout path even if no specific program covers it ([SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/microsoft-will-expand-bug-bounties-even-on-programs-without-official-payouts/)).

**Meta** uses a hybrid model: submissions go through its own portal at [bugbounty.meta.com/report](https://bugbounty.meta.com/scope/), with HackerOne and Bugcrowd handling payment processing only. Scope includes Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, Meta AI, and Meta Quest hardware; the minimum reward is $500. The highest tier — WhatsApp Private Processing — pays up to **$300,000** and is invitation-only. Meta was also the first program to explicitly cover data scraping and AI/LLM vulnerabilities ([TechZine](https://www.techzine.eu/news/privacy-compliance/69870/meta-platforms-inc-expands-its-bug-bounty-program/)).

**Samsung's** self-hosted program at [security.samsung.com](https://news.samsung.com/sg/samsung-electronics-scales-up-mobile-security-rewards-programme-to-boost-industry-collaboration-and-safety) raised its maximum to **$1 million** in 2024 under its Important Scenario Vulnerability Programme, targeting zero-click exploits with persistence on flagship Galaxy devices. Base rewards start at $200. Samsung has paid nearly $5 million in total since 2017 ([SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/samsung-bug-bounty-program-payouts-reach-5m-top-reward-increased-to-1m/)).

**Mozilla** routes reports through its own Bugzilla tracker at [bugzilla.mozilla.org](https://www.mozilla.org/en-US/security/client-bug-bounty/), covering Firefox across all platforms. Top payout is **$20,000** for sandbox escapes or WebExtension install prompt bypasses; UXSS pays up to $10,000; and researchers get a 50% bonus when they demonstrate an exploit mitigation bypass without privileged access. **Tencent's** Security Response Center at [en.security.tencent.com](https://en.security.tencent.com/policy) covers WeChat, QQ, Tencent Cloud, and most major Tencent products with rewards paid in USD (historical top award cited at $15,000 ([Threatpost](https://threatpost.com/tencent-top-bug-bounty-award-15k/154816))). **Alibaba** runs its own ASRC (Alibaba Security Response Center) at [security.alibaba.com](https://hackerone.com/alibaba/policy_versions) alongside a presence on HackerOne for international researchers.

## Intigriti serves European enterprise, hardware, and finance programs

Intigriti (Belgian-founded, UK-headquartered) hosts roughly 400 total programs with approximately 74 confirmed public paid programs. Its roster skews heavily toward European companies but includes notable global names, and critical payouts range from a few hundred euros at small firms to **$100,000 at Intel** (now suspended; see below).

Among the most significant **global tech programs** on Intigriti: **AMD's Product Security Bug Bounty** at [app.intigriti.com/programs/amd](https://app.intigriti.com/programs/amd/amd/detail) pays $500–$10,000 for software bugs and up to **$30,000 for hardware critical findings** ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/pro/security/amd-launches-bug-bounty-program-with-some-seriously-big-rewards)), though AMD faced backlash in 2026 after denying a bounty for a critical auto-updater RCE ([SC World](https://www.scworld.com/brief/amd-faces-backlash-over-denied-bug-bounty-and-changed-disclosure-rules/)). **Adobe's Public Bug Bounty** runs $75–**$15,000**. **NVIDIA's Public Bug Bounty** pays $150–**$15,000**. **Yahoo's Bug Bounty** pays $100–**$10,000**. **Robinhood** pays $100–**$10,000** at [app.intigriti.com/programs/robinhood/robinhoodbbp](https://app.intigriti.com/programs/robinhood/robinhoodbbp). **Grafana Labs** runs a paid program at $100–**$7,500** alongside a separate public VDP ([CSO Online](https://www.csoonline.com/article/4085288/11-top-bug-bounty-programs-launched-in-2024.html)). **DigitalOcean** pays $100–**$5,000** and its Cloudways brand pays $100–**$1,800**. **Rivian** pays $150–**$5,000**. **Altera** (Intel subsidiary) pays $500–**$15,000** (launched January 2025). **WP Engine** pays £0–£1,000 and **Monzo** (UK neobank) pays £200–**£8,250** ([CSO Online](https://www.csoonline.com/article/4085288/11-top-bug-bounty-programs-launched-in-2024.html)).

**European companies** on Intigriti represent a distinct vertical. **BMW Group Automotive** pays €500–**€10,000**; **Skoda Auto** pays €200–**€3,000**; both scope connected-car APIs and web portals. **Visma** (Norwegian SaaS) pays €100–**€3,000** with web and mobile scope ([app.intigriti.com/programs/visma/visma](https://app.intigriti.com/programs/visma/visma)). **TrueLayer** (EU/UK open banking) pays €175–**€4,000**. **Wolt** (Finnish food delivery) pays €150–**€3,000**. **Personio** (German HR SaaS) pays €175–**€3,000**. **SBB** (Swiss Federal Railways) pays €100–**€2,000**. **EURid** (European Registry for Internet Domains) pays €75–**€3,000**. The **Port of Antwerp-Bruges** — a piece of critical European infrastructure — pays €100–**€3,500**, while **Water-Link** (Belgian water utility) pays €100–**€3,000**, reflecting Belgium's notable push to bring critical infrastructure into vulnerability disclosure ([HuggingFace Bug Bounty Dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/lesis-lat/bug-bounty-programs-rewards/blob/main/programs-data.csv) for all tiers). The healthcare sector also appears: **UZ Leuven** (Belgian university hospital) pays €100–**€4,000**, and **Nexuzhealth** pays €0–€2,000.

Intigriti previously hosted the European Commission's open-source bug bounty, which paid up to **€5,000 per vulnerability** plus a 20% bonus for submitting a code fix ([PortSwigger Daily Swig](https://portswigger.net/daily-swig/european-commission-launches-new-open-source-software-bug-bounty-program)). That contract has since moved to YesWeHack (see below). Intel's program on Intigriti — which once paid $2,000–**$100,000** — was suspended in January 2026 after Intel cited a flood of AI-assisted, low-quality submissions ([Tweaktown](https://www.tweaktown.com/news/113651/intel-kills-its-bug-bounty-program-that-paid-up-to-100-000-for-flagging-security-vulnerabilities/index.html)).

## YesWeHack dominates French government and EU institutional programs

YesWeHack (French, founded 2015) hosts 73+ confirmed public paid programs with an exceptional concentration of French public-sector clients and a rapidly growing EU government portfolio. In October 2025, it won the European Commission's four-year framework bug bounty contract worth up to **€7,679,875** ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251001342504/en/YesWeHack-Won-the-European-Commissions-Latest-Bug-Bounty-Tender)), making it the de facto platform for EU institutional vulnerability research.

French government programs on YesWeHack include: **DINUM** (French Digital Interministerial Directorate) covering web apps and APIs with a max of **€5,000** for credential exposure ([CSO Online](https://www.csoonline.com/article/4085288/11-top-bug-bounty-programs-launched-in-2024.html)); **Mon Espace Santé** (national health portal) paying €50–**€4,000**; **FranceConnect / ProConnect** (national digital identity) paying €0–**€3,000**; **Tchap** (government secure messaging) paying €100–**€3,000**; **Démarches-simplifiées** (government forms) paying €50–**€1,500**; **Sante Publique France** paying €50–**€700**; and **Cybermalveillance.gouv.fr** paying €50–**€1,000**. The **French Ministry of Armed Forces** is a confirmed YesWeHack client but its program is not publicly accessible ([YesWeHack RSA Conference 2025](https://www.yeswehack.com/page/yeswehack-at-rsa-conference-2025)). Germany's **Sovereign Tech Agency** funds open-source bounties on YesWeHack including **Log4j** (€100–€5,000), **Systemd** (€250–**€5,000**), **GNOME** (€500–**€5,000**), **OpenPGP.js** (€250–**€5,000**), and **Sequoia PGP** (€500–**€5,000**). The **Government of Quebec** also runs a public program at $50–**$1,500** USD.

The most extraordinary single YesWeHack program is **Swiss Post's e-voting program** at €100–**€70,000** for critical findings — an outlier driven by the existential stakes of vulnerabilities in national election infrastructure. Swiss Post has paid nearly **CHF 1 million** in total bounties ([Netzwoche](https://www.netzwoche.ch/news/2025-07-16/update-post-hat-bug-jaegern-schon-fast-eine-million-franken-ausbezahlt)). Swiss Post's general program separately pays €100–**€5,000**.

Notable French private-sector programs on YesWeHack: **Doctolib** (healthtech) €0–**€4,000**; **TeamViewer** €100–**€4,000**; **FDJ United** (gambling) €50–**€2,500**; **SNCF Connect** (railways) €0–**€2,000**; **OVH** (cloud) €100–**€1,000**; **BlaBlaCar** €50–**€1,000**; **Deezer** €0–**€800**; **Decathlon** €50–**€1,000**; **Qwant** €100–**€2,000** ([HuggingFace Bug Bounty Dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/lesis-lat/bug-bounty-programs-rewards/blob/main/programs-data.csv)). **Ant Group Security Response Center** (Alibaba's Lazada subsidiary) runs on YesWeHack at $10–**$1,100** ([HuggingFace Dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/lesis-lat/bug-bounty-programs-rewards/blob/main/programs-data.csv)). **Louis Vuitton Malletier** runs on YesWeHack at €50–**€1,800**, illustrating the platform's reach into luxury retail. **Singapore GovTech's VDP** (no monetary reward) also appears on YesWeHack with 677 reports as of September 2025 ([YesWeHack GovTech VDP](https://yeswehack.com/programs/vdp)).

## Banks and governments mostly run VDPs, with a few paying exceptions

The financial sector's bug bounty adoption is thin. A 2024 Wavestone benchmark found only **5% of the world's top 100 banks** ran a public paid bug bounty, while 26% had a VDP ([Wavestone RiskInsight 2024](https://www.riskinsight-wavestone.com/en/2024/02/bug-bounty-insight-and-benchmark-on-the-banking-and-public-sectors-2024/)). The Netherlands achieved 100% VDP adoption among its top banks, while in Asia and South America, only one bank among 43 surveyed had any VDP.

Among the notable financial programs: **JPMorgan Chase** runs a responsible disclosure program at [chase.com/digital/resources/privacy-security/security/vulnerability-disclosure](https://www.chase.com/digital/resources/privacy-security/security/vulnerability-disclosure) through a Synack-powered portal at `responsibledisclosure.jpmorganchase.com` — but this is unpaid and managed by Synack rather than self-hosted ([JPMC Responsible Disclosure](https://responsibledisclosure.jpmorganchase.com/hc/en-us)). **Wells Fargo** hosts a self-hosted policy page but routes actual submissions through HackerOne with no stated payout. **DBS Bank (Singapore)** maintains a self-hosted VDP at [dbs.com/vdp](https://www.dbs.com/vdp/default.page) with no confirmed bounty. **Goldman Sachs** and **Citibank** both use HackerOne; **HSBC** uses Bugcrowd.

For government programs, the US **DoD Vulnerability Disclosure Program** covers all publicly accessible DoD information systems and has processed over 50,000 reports since 2016 — but pays **no bounties** for the continuous VDP ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/pro/us-department-of-defense-claims-to-have-flushed-out-50000-vulnerabilities-with-bug-bounty-program)). Separate "Hack the Pentagon" challenges on HackerOne are paid events. The **CISA VDP Platform** (operated with Bugcrowd) covers 50+ US Federal Civilian Executive Branch agencies unpaid, though two agencies ran paid pilots in 2023 averaging **~$1,500 per accepted submission** ([Bugcrowd](https://www.bugcrowd.com/blog/cisas-vdp-platform-annual-report-explained/)). The UK **NCSC Vulnerability Reporting Service** (via HackerOne, since 2018) and **NHS England**'s VDP (also HackerOne) pay nothing. The **Welsh Government** published its own VDP on December 12, 2025 via HackerOne.

The most sophisticated government paying program in Asia Pacific belongs to **Singapore GovTech**, which runs three tiers: an unpaid public VDP, a seasonal paid Government Bug Bounty Programme, and a year-round **Vulnerability Rewards Programme** paying US$250–$5,000 standard with up to **US$150,000 for exceptional-impact findings** on selected systems ([SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/singapores-govtech-announces-new-vulnerability-rewards-programme/)). **South Korea's Financial Security Institute** has run a financial-sector bounty since 2019, expanded in 2022 to mobile apps, with prizes up to **~10 million KRW (~$7,500 USD)** ([Asiae](https://view.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2022080310050053798)).

## Web3 platforms offer the industry's highest absolute payouts

**Immunefi** is the dominant Web3 bug bounty platform, hosting **287+ active programs** with over $162 million in total available bounties and more than **$110 million paid out** since founding ([Immunefi](https://immunefi.com/hackers/)). The largest single payout in software history was **Wormhole's $10 million** award to whitehat satya0x; Aurora paid **$6 million** to pwning.eth; Polygon paid **$2.2 million** and Optimism paid **$2 million** ([Immunefi](https://immunefi.com/hackers/)). Active high-paying programs include **MakerDAO/Sky** at $10 million max, **Ethena** at $3 million max, **Arbitrum** at $2 million max (since August 2021), **Scroll** at $1 million, and **Kamino** (Solana's largest at launch) at $1.5 million. The **Ethereum Foundation** lists up to $50,000 on Immunefi for infrastructure bugs, with a $10,000 minimum for critical smart contract flaws. Immunefi charges a 10% commission on payouts and is fully open to all researchers.

**HackenProof** is Immunefi's main Web3 competitor with **400+ active programs**, hosting the **Ethereum Foundation**, OKX, Starknet (web/SC at $250,000; blockchain/DLT at $250,000), **Sui Foundation**, **NEAR Protocol**, **Gate.io**, **Avalanche**, and others ([HackenProof](https://hackenproof.com/what-is-hackenproof)). Top program on the current listing is NAVI Protocol at **$300,000** max. Robinhood's separate **Robinhood Chain** bounty runs on **Cantina** (a newer smart-contract-focused platform) with a maximum of **$1,000,000** starting September 2026 ([Cantina](https://cantina.xyz/bounties/55316f42-3c5e-4746-9bd0-0f18dcbc344b)). Coinbase also uses Cantina for its onchain/Base network program alongside its HackerOne program.

**Synack** operates as a vetted security testing platform rather than an open bug bounty. Fewer than 10% of applicants are accepted to the Synack Red Team, and all programs are private. Synack runs **responsibledisclosure.com** for public, unpaid VDP submissions on behalf of its enterprise and government clients including the US DoD ([Synack](https://www.synack.com/synack-red-team/)). **Cobalt.io** is explicitly a "Pentest as a Service" company and not a bug bounty platform; it serves over 1,500 companies including Credit Karma, Axel Springer, and Santa Cruz County Bank with structured, time-boxed pentesting engagements rather than open bounties ([Cobalt.io](https://www.cobalt.io/solutions/cobalt-vs-bug-bounty-providers)). **Standoff Bug Bounty** (by Positive Technologies) grew to **233 programs** in 2025 (2.2x increase) with 32,000 registered researchers, primarily serving the Russian/CIS market ([Positive Technologies](https://global.ptsecurity.com/en/about/news/standoff-bug-bounty-year-end-results-32-of-finds-are-high-and-critical-level-vulnerabilities/)).

## Conclusion

The alternative bug bounty ecosystem is no longer marginal. Google, Apple, and Microsoft collectively paid over **$50 million** through their own portals in roughly a 12-month window, dwarfing what many third-party platforms distribute. For researchers targeting traditional software, the self-hosted programs offer the highest ceilings — Apple's $5M+ bonus potential and Google's $1.5M Android top tier are unmatched anywhere. For European programs, Intigriti and YesWeHack together represent a sovereign European security research market: Intigriti has become the platform for Belgian and Northern European enterprise (including critical infrastructure), while YesWeHack has locked in the French government and, with the EU Commission's €7.7M contract, now anchors European institutional security research. The most consequential development in bounties, however, is the Web3 sector: Immunefi's $10M MakerDAO program and multi-million-dollar historical payouts have permanently reset expectations about what vulnerability research can pay, and that ceiling only moves upward as DeFi protocol TVL grows. The practical implication for researchers: program selection should be platform-agnostic, and the highest-value targets increasingly sit outside the two platforms that dominate casual awareness.
